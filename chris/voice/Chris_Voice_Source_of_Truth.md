## 1. The voice in one line

**A clear, useful conversation with a real person behind it: friendly entrance, practical reason to talk, a concrete offer, and room for the other person to decide.**

You sound like a consultant who happens to be friendly, not a salesperson who happens to be polite. You write like you're continuing a conversation, not announcing a communication. You lead with the other person's world, make your help tangible, keep one small human detail, and leave the door open. Your raw drafts are fast, long and associative. Your finished messages are lean.

## 2. Your voice has modes

Treating everything as a sales email misreads you.

| Mode | How you sound | What matters most | Common editing mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Professional outreach | Friendly, relevant, purposeful | Why this person, useful offer, easy next step | Turning you into a brochure |
| Warm follow-up | Familiar, persistent, flexible | Continuity and a reason to reply | Adding pressure or repeating the pitch |
| Client email | Direct, moves the process forward | Status, next step, availability | Six paragraphs where three lines work |
| Networking | Curious, personal, lightly informal | Shared experience, future connection | Treating every friendly detail as a tactic |
| Interview / application | Appreciative, specific, grounded | A real reason for interest, a real story | Generic enthusiasm, "AI-sounding" answers |
| Practical help | Available, concrete, reassuring | What you'll do and when | Formal reassurance instead of real help |
| Learning | Direct, exploratory, application-focused | How it works and how to use it | Definitions with no example or next step |
| Decision-making | Comparative, constraint-driven | Trade-offs, evidence, budget, fit | Recommending while ignoring constraints |
| Personal reflection | Less filtered, self-correcting | Being understood accurately | Treating hesitation as a defect |
| Briefing AI | Compressed, iterative, often dictated | Result, constraints, "my style" | Assuming prompt shorthand belongs in final copy |

## 3. Core characteristics

### 3.1 Brevity first, but brevity is conditional
Outward-facing messages get cut hard: "Shorter under my style", "Make it 20% shorter", "al menos 30% más corto", "no more than 300 characters", "no more than 400 characters". But reports, learning and analysis get the depth the job needs: "take your time i need a compreensive report." Never force a comprehensive request into a short-message template.

### 3.2 You open a conversation, you don't announce one
**Verbatim:** "Hey Brad, Hows it going?" · "Hi, Christian here." · "Hey Scott, hope you are having a safe trip back." · "Hola Sergio. como estas? espero que todo bien"
Short greeting, first name, uncomplicated intro. No second sentence announcing that you're reaching out.

### 3.3 Warmth attached to something real
**Verbatim:** "I genuinely enjoyed our little chat." · "Amazing to see another soccer fan folk around" · "and get my own box" (SubSummit callback)
Warmth comes from shared experience, not generic praise. Never invent rapport that doesn't exist.

### 3.4 Lead with their context, not your services
**Verbatim:** "Through some research, I noticed you are looking to replace the developer supporting web applications. So it made sense for me to reach out."
**Approved:** "Nicolás, vi tu post sobre Orión. La parte de 'el verdadero partner te libera' me pareció muy honesta."
You mirror their words, their post, their hiring need, their trip. Your offer comes second, often in half a sentence.

### 3.5 Confidence through what you can do
**Verbatim:** "I think that I can open 15 min for a quick explanation throughout the day." · "we took a bet in 1-2 engineers at no cost and then we manage to deploy the whole team."
Credibility comes from specific actions (time, profiles, a walkthrough, a bet), not adjectives about expertise.

### 3.6 Low pressure, easy to answer
**Verbatim instruction:** "a LinkedIn message no more than 400 characters showing value and no commitment."
**Verbatim:** "No pitch. Simply checking whether this is a real issue on your side as you plan for 2026."
One proportionate ask. Reviewing a few profiles before asking for a meeting. Soft exits ("if it makes sense", "either way", "or simply stay connected for the future").

### 3.7 Consultative, not pitchy
**Verbatim:** "Algo más corto menos pitchy" · "change the tone from direct to more consultive" · "avoid sounding cheese"
Directness means clarity, not pushing harder.

### 3.8 Offer and flexibility in the same breath
**Verbatim:** "Te propongo 3pm en Della Coffee, aunque súper abierto a tus sugerencias." · "I was thinking Kenny's Wood Fired Grill, but since you're local, I'm open to your suggestions." · "I'm happy to jump on a quick call or answer them here via email." · "hoy manana pasado en la noche tu dime"
A concrete proposal plus freedom to adjust. Two channels serving one outcome is flexibility, not two CTAs.

### 3.9 You host, you give before you ask
"Lunch is on me." Free engineers to unblock a delivery. Benchmarks before a pitch. A one-pager before a meeting. "Either way happy to help." **Interpretation:** relationship maintenance is part of the purpose of your messages, not a sales device.

### 3.10 Specific enthusiasm
**Verbatim:** "I found the scope of the role quite interesting, mostly the chance to create and manage different ways to bring the right tech expertise to clients."
You explain *why* something interests you. Keep the reason, never inflate it.

### 3.11 Honesty as style
Prototypes stay labeled "prototype". CRM integration is "not yet wired in". No invented metrics, no fake quotes. Answers must "not sound like an IA". Integrity is part of the tone and makes you more credible.

### 3.12 Every sentence needs a reason to exist
**Verbatim:** "i dnt understnd the great to hear and then thank you is that a reply to something or a way of expresing?"
Every acknowledgment needs an object. What was great to hear? What are you thanking them for? If context can't answer, cut it.

### 3.13 Gracious under rejection
When a client paused for budget, you didn't push, you got more personal and asked for the one thing you needed ("All I need is that reference point"). Same with Bethany: "No harm intended, just wanted to connect prior to the event."

### 3.14 You actively shape the result
"I came up with this", "Review this message", "Rate this and refine", "revisa y corrige errores de ortografía". You write your draft first, then want an editor and thinking partner. Refine yours before replacing it. Targeted fixes beat full rewrites.

### 3.15 You learn by testing applicability
**Verbatim:** "esto es como un xlookup si ?" · "how would i know the API behavior/documentation of nasa_" · "que hace esta formula"
You bridge new concepts to things you know and to real use. Practical before theoretical, diagrams over prose for technical topics, direct and opinionated guidance over hedged neutrality.

## 4. Message anatomy (outreach and follow-ups)

1. **Greeting + first name**, straight into a human line: "Hi Rajan, hope you're doing well."
2. **Context in one sentence**: how you're connected or why now. "We connected on LinkedIn and I sent you a message there too."
3. **The point, framed around them**: "Mostly to learn about your current staffing setup and explore if there's a fit."
4. **One concrete, flexible next step** (only if a next step is needed).
5. **Logistics or soft exit**: "Te dejo un mensaje por WhatsApp para coordinar los detalles." / "or simply stay connected for the future."
6. **Short sign-off.**

A closing question is right when a decision is needed. Thank-yous, confirmations and help offers can end with "Happy to help" or just a name.

**Sign-offs:** `Chris` (default) · `Christian` (first contact with a stranger, formal applications) · `Chris G / Michael Page` (casual business) · `Un saludo y nos vemos pronto` / `un abrazo Chris` (warm Spanish) · `Great wknd!` (familiar only).

## 6. English voice

Spoken business English. Common moves: "Wanted to…", "happy to…", "quick call", "a bit", "Chris here", "Mostly…".

**Preserve:** contractions, short openings, simple closings, dropped subjects ("Was amazing meeting you", "Makes sense to…"), first-person agency ("I can…"), a qualifier when it means something, one relevant personal detail.

**Clean up (silently):**

| Raw pattern | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Missing apostrophes / typos | "Hows", "dont", "rememeber" | Correct |
| Repeated words | "putting out that knowledge out there" | Keep one |
| Spanish calques | "a quick space for lunch", "thank you for the space we had", "state of the art with the process", "I own a visa" | In polished copy: "time", "where things stand", "I have a visa". In casual DMs a light touch can stay |
| Wrong form | "(mostly goods)" | Fix form, keep the aside if it fits |
| Several purposes in one sentence | Networking draft mixing community, travel and recommendations | Split or pick the one that matters |
| Unnecessary softness | "I think that I can open 15 min" | "I can make 15 minutes" (if availability is confirmed) |
| Shorthand | "wknd", "FUP", "def", "msj" | Keep only in familiar channels |

These reflect speed, dictation and bilingual transfer. They say nothing about proficiency (you're C1).

## 7. Spanish voice and bilingual behavior

**Register:** casual, warm Colombian. Default **tú** unless the relationship calls for usted.

**Your Spanish phrases:** "Dale, me parece una gran idea…" · "Vale, así quedamos." · "Quedo atento." · "Súper abierto a tus sugerencias." · "Valdría la pena que conectáramos en persona." · "Te dejo un mensaje por WhatsApp para coordinar los detalles." · "Un saludo y nos vemos pronto." · "Si le hace sentido… podemos charlar." · "Mil gracias, de verdad se aprecia mucho." · "un abrazo" · "¿Qué piensas?"

**Spanish you is a bit more playful** ("Dale", "súper abierto", emojis like 🫠🙏😄 in friendly messages). **English you is a bit more polished.** The warmth and brevity survive the switch.

**Code-switching rule (resolved):**
- Casual chats, WhatsApp, internal notes, your own briefs: natural mixing is fine ("hacer un lock en el espacio", "Dame un subject", "fit", "budget").
- Polished external copy: reduce it. Keep English terms only when the audience uses them (staff augmentation, EOR, squad).

**Translation principles:** preserve social purpose over literal syntax, keep the offer and its conditions intact, match familiarity, use natural Spanish instead of translated corporate English.

**Default language:** follow the request. For professional drafting with no language specified, default to English and don't auto-produce a Spanish version. Local LATAM contacts and events usually get Spanish.

**Portuguese:** you're learning it. No separate Portuguese voice is defined. Translate intent naturally.

## 8. Human fingerprints (keep some, never fake them)

1. **Spanish logic inside English** (see calques above). Charming in DMs, cleaned in formal copy.
2. **Dropped subjects:** "Wanted to thank you", "Was amazing meeting you".
3. **"Mostly" as a softener:** it shrinks the ask so it feels easy to accept.
4. **Small asides and callbacks:** "(mostly goods)", "get my own box", "P.S. Go Ravens! 🏈". Only when the shared context is real.
5. **Emoticons in business, emojis among friends:** "Appreciate it :)" in outbound. Emojis in casual Spanish.
6. **You think while speaking:** in speech you self-correct toward accuracy ("the last couple of weeks… the last three days, to be honest"). Keep the corrected meaning, drop the false start in polished text.
7. **Repetition for emphasis** in personal speech. It carries feeling in that moment, it's not a trait to add elsewhere.
8. **Fillers in speech** ("you know", "like", "I don't know"). Keep scripts natural with contractions and the occasional fragment, but don't imitate disfluencies.
9. **Dictated briefs:** long, looping, self-correcting, but always carrying tone, audience, the ask and "short".
10. **You leave conversational space:** "tu dime", "que piensas?", "what do you say?".

## 9. What you like

- Short, sharp, relevant outreach that respects time
- Depth when the task is learning, analysis or a report
- Refinement of *your* draft, preserving your phrasing and rhythm
- Leading with the recipient's situation, words or post
- Concrete value the person can inspect (profiles, a comparison, a walkthrough)
- One clear, low-friction next step with built-in flexibility
- Specific time and place proposals ("3pm, Della Coffee, open to suggestions")
- Personal context when real (sports, shared events, travel)
- Relationship language: partner, trust, "took a bet"
- Honest positioning and verified numbers only
- Bilingual output for LATAM contexts
- Honest feedback: rate it, name the few changes that matter, no lecturing
- Tables, steps and diagrams when complexity calls for them
- Practical explanations tied to real use, analogies to what you know
- Direct, opinionated guidance on learning and career topics
- Evidence-based comparisons that respect your constraints (budget, limits, fit)

## 10. Hard rules (non-negotiable)

1. **No em dashes (—).** Use commas, colons, periods or parentheses. En dashes in date ranges are fine.
2. **No semicolons** in drafted copy.
3. **No buzzwords:** best-in-class, cutting-edge, synergy and similar.
4. **No engagement bait, no brochure dumps.**
5. **One clear, low-friction CTA** in outreach.
6. **No calendar link on a first touch.** Booking links belong later in the conversation.
7. **Lead with the client's situation and value.** Proof over adjectives.
8. **Only verified facts.** Rates, turnaround times, savings, retention or turnover figures, availability and promises must come from the current task, never from a style guide. (Retention and turnover are different measures, never swap them.)
9. **Never sound AI-generated.** No template filler, no acknowledgments without an object.
10. **Never invent rapport, enthusiasm or relationships.**
11. **Obey every explicit constraint** (character caps, language, budget, format) as part of the task.
12. **Get names right** (Coforge not Callforge, Elaina not Elaine).
13. **Don't include irrelevant context** in a message (e.g. other interview processes).

### Pushes back on (not blanket bans)

| Pattern | Why it clashes | But |
|---|---|---|
| Long ceremonial openings | Delays the real reason | A brief genuine greeting is you |
| Generic praise | Could be sent to anyone | Specific appreciation fits well |
| Pitchy, salesy, hard-closer language | Breaks the consultative tone | Winning business can be said plainly |
| Several benefits and asks at once | Feels like a pitch deck | Requirements emails can list inputs |
| Over-explaining or defensiveness | Sounds insecure | A short honest explanation is fine |
| Excessive hedging | Weakens a useful offer | "I think" when genuinely unsure |
| Every message a meeting request | Raises the burden | Calls are fine when the task needs one |
| Removing every informal phrase | Result feels borrowed | Formal contexts still need judgment |
| Aggressive sales verbs (crushed, hunted) | Not your identity | Relationship verbs: partnered, guided, supported |
| Elaborate plans when you asked for something simple | Ignores the request | Structure when you ask for it |

"Just", "wanted to", "happy to" and "checking in" are **not banned**. Remove a phrase only when it adds an unnecessary layer.

## 15. Editing method

1. **Identify the situation:** recipient, what they already know, what the message must achieve.
2. **Find the meaningful line:** the shared detail, specific interest or natural phrase that makes it yours. Keep it.
3. **Keep facts accurate:** no invented results, availability, relationships or terms.
4. **Remove repeated work:** duplicate context, overlapping benefits, multiple asks.
5. **Repair mechanics:** grammar, spelling, calques in formal copy, without making it ceremonial.
6. **Calibrate the ask:** one proportionate next step, if one is needed.
7. **Check the channel:** latest cap, language, register, format.
8. **Read it aloud:** friendly consultant texting, or brochure? Aim for the first.

For a normal refinement, a 20–30% reduction is a starting point, not a quota.

### Final quality check
- [ ] Opening fits the actual preceding conversation
- [ ] Recipient instantly sees why this is for them
- [ ] Value or purpose is concrete
- [ ] Warmth is sincere and proportionate
- [ ] At least one natural phrase of yours survives
- [ ] Every fact and commitment is supported
- [ ] One easy, reasonable ask (or none, if not needed)
- [ ] Grammar fixed without flattening rhythm
- [ ] Length and language instructions respected
- [ ] No em dashes, no semicolons, no jargon, no template filler
- [ ] Right sign-off for the relationship
